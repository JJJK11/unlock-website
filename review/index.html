OpenAI Launches Deployment Company With Bain, Goldman Sachs, and SoftBank as Enterprise AI Adoption Hits a Tipping Point

OpenAI on May 11 launched the OpenAI Deployment Company, a new entity designed to help organizations build and deploy AI systems they can rely on every day across their most important work. The launch includes the acquisition of applied AI consulting firm Tomoro and is structured as a partnership with 19 investment and consultancy firms including Bain, Goldman Sachs, and SoftBank, while remaining majority owned and controlled by OpenAI. OpenAI Chief Revenue Officer Denise Dresser said enterprise adoption has reached a tipping point, with enterprise now more than 40% of OpenAI revenue and on track to reach parity with consumer by the end of 2026. The move places OpenAI directly into the enterprise transformation and integration market alongside the largest professional services and systems integrators.

The launch also reframes the competitive landscape. Anthropic recently partnered with Goldman Sachs and Blackstone to launch a $1.5 billion firm targeting faster Anthropic AI adoption across hundreds of companies, and Google continues to scale its own enterprise transformation motion. OpenAI's strategic shift toward Amazon Web Services Bedrock also expands the surface where enterprise buyers can reach the latest OpenAI models without exclusive Microsoft Azure routing. For enterprise technology buyers, the practical implication is that the integration and deployment layer between foundation models and operating processes is now a contested market, with new vendor combinations, pricing patterns, and contractual options to evaluate inside 2026 and 2027 planning cycles.

  • OpenAI Deployment Company launched May 11 with 19 partner firms including Bain, Goldman Sachs, and SoftBank; majority owned by OpenAI
  • OpenAI acquired applied AI consulting firm Tomoro as part of the launch
  • Enterprise now over 40% of OpenAI revenue, expected to reach parity with consumer by end of 2026
  • Anthropic separately partnered with Goldman Sachs and Blackstone on a $1.5B firm for Anthropic enterprise deployment

Enterprise Impact: CIO, CTO, and transformation leadership should treat the deployment layer between foundation models and operating processes as a contested market in 2026 and 2027 planning, with OpenAI Deployment Company, Anthropic and Goldman Sachs and Blackstone, Google, and the established systems integrator base all competing for the same scope of work. Procurement should benchmark integrator pricing, model access terms, and AI governance commitments across multiple vendor combinations before committing to a multi year deployment engagement. ISO/IEC 42001 alignment should be a baseline requirement for any deployment partner asked to operate inside the enterprise. Canadian buyers should pair vendor decisions with explicit residency, lawful access, and data egress language, particularly where the deployment partner is routing inference through US controlled infrastructure.

Source: CNBC

Canadian Bank Executives and Federal Regulators Meet on Risks Raised by Anthropic's Latest AI Model

Canadian bank executives and federal regulators met this week to discuss cybersecurity risks raised by Anthropic's latest AI model, which has been flagged for capability that could help threat actors identify and exploit software vulnerabilities at unprecedented speed. The meeting takes place against the backdrop of the same week European Central Bank warning to financial institutions and reflects the operational risk framing that Canadian prudential supervisors are bringing to AI threat exposure.

  • Canadian bank executives met federal regulators May 9 on AI model risk tied to Anthropic frontier capability
  • Discussion parallels the ECB warning the same week to European financial institutions
  • Signals Canadian supervisory direction on AI threat exposure inside operational resilience expectations

Enterprise Impact: Enterprises deploying frontier AI in production should expect customer assurance and regulator conversations to expand to cover both how AI is being used inside the organization and how the organization is preparing for AI assisted threat exposure. CIO and CISO teams should align procurement, vendor risk, and operational resilience programs to support that dual conversation. ISO/IEC 42001 alignment supports defender side and developer side AI governance, while ISO/IEC 27001 provides the underlying information security baseline. Canadian buyers should request explicit residency and lawful access terms from any frontier AI vendor under negotiation.

Source: The Globe and Mail

Google Threat Intelligence: AI Assisted Hacking Has Reached Industrial Scale Across China, Russia, and North Korea Linked Actors

Google Threat Intelligence Group reported this week that AI assisted hacking activity has evolved into an industrial scale threat within months. Threat actors tied to China, Russia, and North Korea are using advanced AI systems to improve malware development, phishing campaign quality, persistence techniques inside compromised environments, and downstream vulnerability exploitation. Google characterised the shift as a near term change in the threat environment that defenders should expect to face in operational settings during 2026.

  • Google Threat Intelligence reports AI assisted hacking at industrial scale across China, Russia, and North Korea linked actors
  • Use cases span malware development, phishing quality, persistence techniques, and vulnerability exploitation
  • Finding aligns with ECB and Palo Alto Networks warnings the same week

Enterprise Impact: CIO and CISO offices should ensure detection engineering, threat hunting, and email security programs are calibrated against AI generated content quality and AI accelerated reconnaissance. ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A.5.7 threat intelligence, A.5.24 incident management planning, and A.8.16 monitoring activities should be reviewed against current threat conditions. ISO/IEC 42001 alignment supports governance of any defender side AI tooling introduced into security operations. Boards should request a single integrated view of AI threat exposure, defender side AI tooling, and operational resilience posture.

Palo Alto Networks: New Anthropic and OpenAI Cybersecurity Models Identified Seven Times the Normal Vulnerabilities in a Month

Palo Alto Networks disclosed this week that new AI cybersecurity models from Anthropic and OpenAI identified more than seven times the normal number of software vulnerabilities in a single month of testing. Security leaders warned that organizations may have only months before similar offensive capability becomes widely available to attackers. The disclosure quantifies the offensive capability gradient that the ECB and Google Threat Intelligence Group also flagged the same week.

  • New Anthropic and OpenAI cybersecurity models identified more than seven times the normal number of vulnerabilities in a single month of testing
  • Palo Alto warned similar offensive capability could reach attackers within months
  • Disclosure quantifies the AI threat acceleration framed by ECB and Google in the same week

Enterprise Impact: Vulnerability and patch management programs should plan to operate at materially faster cadence. ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A.8.8 management of technical vulnerabilities, A.8.25 secure development lifecycle, A.8.28 secure coding, and A.8.29 security testing in development and acceptance should be reviewed for capacity to keep pace with AI assisted exploitation. Boards should request evidence that the organization's secure development and application security testing program is calibrated against current threat tempo, not the 2024 baseline. ISO/IEC 42001 alignment supports governance of any AI tooling introduced into security operations.

European Central Bank Issues Strongest Warning Yet on AI Enabled Cyberattacks Against Financial Institutions

The European Central Bank this week urged financial institutions to urgently prepare for AI enabled cyberattacks tied to frontier models, including the Mythos class of capability. The ECB stated that AI systems can identify and chain software vulnerabilities significantly faster than traditional approaches and that regulated institutions must elevate AI threat preparation inside operational resilience programs. The statement is one of the strongest public warnings yet from a major regulator on enterprise AI risk management and operational governance obligations.

  • ECB urged financial institutions to urgently prepare for frontier AI enabled cyberattacks on May 13
  • Regulators flagged that AI systems can identify and chain vulnerabilities significantly faster than traditional approaches
  • Sets the regulatory tone for how prudential supervisors are addressing AI threat exposure

Enterprise Impact: Regulated financial institutions and any organization inside critical infrastructure or federal supply chains should treat the ECB warning as a leading indicator of Canadian and US supervisory expectations. ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A.5.7 threat intelligence, A.5.24 incident management planning, A.8.7 protection against malware, A.8.8 management of technical vulnerabilities, and A.8.16 monitoring activities frame the management system response. ISO/IEC 42001:2023 extends the response to defender side AI governance. Boards should request evidence that threat intelligence intake, vulnerability cadence, and detection engineering contemplate AI assisted offensive capability. Operational resilience tabletop exercises should add an explicit AI threat scenario through the rest of 2026.

Source: BankInfoSecurity
