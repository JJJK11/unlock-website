BDC Launches the $500 Million LIFT Program to Move Canadian SMEs From AI Intent to Production

The Business Development Bank of Canada launched LIFT on April 24, a $500 million financing and advisory program designed to move more than 1,000 Canadian small and mid sized enterprises from thinking about AI to operating it in production. LIFT, short for Lead with Innovation and Focus on Technology, pairs eligible business owners with expert AI advisors and offers loans from $25,000 to $5 million with flexible repayment, including the option to postpone principal payments for up to two years. Eligible spend covers digital tools, data infrastructure, cybersecurity, Canadian AI applications, and advanced equipment including automation and robotics.

The market problem LIFT is solving is concrete. BDC reporting cited that only 30 percent of Canadian SMEs used AI in 2025, but those that did were 24 percent more productive than their peers. The program prioritizes Canadian developed AI tools and equipment with explicit incentives to adopt domestic solutions, alongside Canadian AI experts to guide implementation. The combination of capital, advisory capacity, and a Canadian first preference is the most coordinated federal AI adoption signal of the year, and arrives at a moment when enterprise AI capital expenditure across the largest US platforms is on track to exceed $700 billion.

  • $500M envelope, loans $25,000 to $5 million, principal postponement up to two years
  • Pairs financing with expert AI advisory support to identify, validate, and implement use cases
  • Eligible spend covers digital tools, data infrastructure, cybersecurity, Canadian AI applications, and automation
  • Explicit preference for Canadian developed AI tools, equipment, and advisory talent

Enterprise Impact: LIFT changes the economics of AI adoption for Canadian SMEs and the mid market. Buyers evaluating AI investments through 2026 should sequence funded programs against the LIFT cycle and the advisory capacity it unlocks, not the standard commercial credit calendar. Vendors selling AI platforms, data infrastructure, and automation into Canada should expect the Canadian first preference to surface in procurement conversations and should be prepared to evidence Canadian operations, residency, and support footprint. Boards should treat LIFT as a directional signal that productivity expectations on AI investments are increasing; the 24 percent productivity differential reported by BDC will become a benchmark in operating reviews. Programs should be governed against ISO/IEC 42001 from inception so that scale up does not require retrofitting AI management.

Source: BDC

BDC Defence Platform Scaled to $6 Billion as Canada Bids to Host the Defence, Security and Resilience Bank

The Business Development Bank of Canada confirmed in late April that its Defence Platform has been scaled to $6 billion after deploying $91.7 million to 16 SMEs since launch, and named the leadership of StrongNorth, a new $300 million venture capital fund for early stage deep tech with defence focused or dual use applications. In parallel, the Department of Finance confirmed on April 28 that progress is being made on the multilateral Defence, Security and Resilience Bank, with Canada bidding to host the headquarters and BDC President and CEO Isabelle Hudon leading the negotiation.

  • Defence Platform scaled to $6B with $91.7M deployed to 16 SMEs since the December launch
  • StrongNorth Fund is a new $300M venture capital vehicle for deep tech with defence or dual use applications
  • Canada actively bidding to host the multilateral Defence, Security and Resilience Bank headquarters

Enterprise Impact: The combined funding signal pulls Canadian dual use technology into a more structured capital pathway than has existed before. Enterprises in advanced manufacturing, AI, cybersecurity, and aerospace should map current capability against defence applicable use cases and engage BDC and StrongNorth dialogue early. Suppliers seeking growth capital should architect their cyber security, controlled goods, and ITAR posture against allied baselines from inception. Procurement teams in defence primes and large enterprises should expect downstream supplier qualification fields to reference CPCSC Level 1 attestation status as a standing requirement.

Source: BDC

Government of Canada Confirms CPCSC Level 1 Path Into Defence Procurement

Public Services and Procurement Canada published reinforced communications around the Canadian Program for Cyber Security Certification Level 1 on May 1, confirming that Level 1 will be required in select defence contracts beginning Summer 2026. Level 1 is a self attestation against 13 baseline security controls technically aligned with NIST SP 800 171 foundational requirements. Level 2, requiring external assessment by accredited certification bodies, is on a spring 2027 timeline. Level 3 will be assessed directly by the Government of Canada for highest risk scenarios.

  • Level 1 self attestation requirement begins in select defence contracts Summer 2026
  • Level 1 controls align with NIST SP 800 171 foundational controls
  • Level 2 external assessment scheduled for select contracts beginning spring 2027

Enterprise Impact: Suppliers should pair CPCSC Level 1 attestation with documented operational evidence of control implementation, not policy presence alone. Existing ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A coverage and NIST SP 800 171 implementations map cleanly onto the Level 1 control set. Enterprises in the defence supply chain should expect Level 1 status to surface as a standing supplier qualification field through 2026 and accelerate Level 2 readiness assessments now to use the runway to 2027 effectively while accredited certification body capacity is being established in the Canadian market.

Source: Government of Canada

Big Tech Q1 2026 Earnings: Alphabet and Amazon Pull Ahead on AI, Combined Capex on Track for $700 Billion

Amazon, Meta, Alphabet, and Microsoft reported Q1 2026 earnings after the close on April 29, with Apple following on April 30. Alphabet’s Google Cloud revenue grew 63 percent year over year, well ahead of the 47 percent consensus, with the cloud backlog nearly doubling to $460 billion. Amazon Web Services grew 28 percent year over year to over $37 billion in revenue, posting its largest sequential quarterly increase ever. Combined 2026 capital expenditure plans across Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, and Meta now exceed $700 billion, nearly double 2025 spend and $100 billion above prior guidance.

  • Google Cloud Q1 revenue up 63 percent year over year, backlog nearly doubled to $460B
  • AWS Q1 revenue up 28 percent year over year to over $37B, largest sequential gain on record
  • Combined 2026 capex across Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta tracking above $700B

Enterprise Impact: The quarter cements that hyperscaler capacity and pricing pressure will continue through 2026 and into 2027. Multi year cloud commitments under negotiation should include capacity reservations, price escalation caps, and named SKU continuity terms for AI workloads. Architecture teams should assume that frontier model capability is consolidating to providers with the deepest AI infrastructure spend, and plan accordingly for both vendor selection and contingency. Canadian buyers should pair platform decisions with explicit residency, lawful access, and data egress language.

Source: Bloomberg

EU AI Act Digital Omnibus Trilogue Ends Without Agreement, August 2026 Deadline Remains Operative

The second political trilogue on the Digital Omnibus between the European Parliament, Council of the EU, and European Commission ended on April 28 without agreement. A further trilogue is scheduled for May 13. The Commission proposal published in November 2025 seeks to defer the high risk AI Act compliance deadline from August 2 2026 to December 2 2027. Until the agreement is published in the Official Journal, the August 2 2026 deadline remains the only operative legal deadline. Member States must still establish at least one national AI regulatory sandbox by August 2 2026.

  • Second trilogue April 28 ended without agreement, third scheduled for May 13
  • August 2 2026 high risk compliance deadline remains operative until agreement is published
  • National AI regulatory sandboxes still required in each Member State by August 2 2026

Enterprise Impact: Enterprises with EU exposure should plan against the August 2 2026 deadline and treat any deferral as upside, not baseline. Anchor AI risk management, post market monitoring, and event logging architectures to ISO/IEC 42001 so that EU specific obligations become a configurable overlay. Customer due diligence on AI governance posture is intensifying regardless of trilogue outcome. Boards should request a single status view of AI regime exposure across EU, Canadian, and US obligations rather than tracking each separately.

Source: European Commission

Itron Confirms Intrusion in Energy Grid Technology Stack, OT Vendor Risk Returns to Board Agendas

Itron, a major supplier of smart meters, grid edge devices, and utility software to electricity, gas, and water utilities, confirmed on April 27 that intruders had been present in its systems before being expelled. Itron has not disclosed the type of attack or the customer impact. The disclosure lands inside a Q1 2026 threat picture in which industry trackers recorded 1,138 publicly claimed ransomware attacks, with healthcare, manufacturing, education, and critical infrastructure absorbing the largest share.

  • Itron supplies smart meters and grid edge software to utilities across North America including Canadian deployments
  • Company has not disclosed attack type, impact scope, or whether customer environments were affected
  • Q1 2026 saw 1,138 publicly claimed ransomware attacks with critical infrastructure heavily represented

Enterprise Impact: Utilities and any organization with Itron components in the operational technology stack should request a structured incident briefing under existing contractual rights and update third party risk records accordingly. Operational technology vendor risk should be monitored at the same cadence as IT vendor risk; ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A 5.21, 5.22, and 5.23 supply chain and cloud security controls apply. Boards of utility owners and large industrial operators should request evidence that OT vendor compromise scenarios are exercised, including supply chain compromise variants in tabletop runs.

Source: TechCrunch

ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO/IEC 27001 Converge as the Baseline Stack for Enterprise AI Assurance

Industry analysis through April converged on a clear pattern: ISO/IEC 27001 is now treated as the foundational information security layer, and ISO/IEC 42001 is the natural overlay for AI specific risk including bias, explainability gaps, model drift, and misuse. Reporting indicates organizations already certified to ISO/IEC 27001 can achieve ISO/IEC 42001 conformance materially faster than greenfield, because the common Annex SL clause structure, Statement of Applicability mechanics, and management review cadence carry across.

  • ISO/IEC 27001 increasingly treated as the foundational layer with ISO/IEC 42001 layered on for AI specific risk
  • ISO/IEC 27001 certified organizations can reach ISO/IEC 42001 conformance materially faster than greenfield
  • Procurement teams in EU, US, and Asia increasingly requesting both standards in vendor RFPs

Enterprise Impact: Enterprises operating AI in production should plan toward dual ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO/IEC 42001 conformance with shared management system mechanics where possible. Statement of Applicability documents should carry both control sets. Internal audit programs should map evidence sources once and use them across both standards. Boards should expect ISO/IEC 42001 to appear in customer due diligence questionnaires through 2026 and 2027 and should treat early conformance as a competitive advantage.

Source: ISO
